Straight answers, because the questions are hard enough already.

Every answer below names who actually makes that decision — us, or someone else.

+ tuition & living, which vary by state and college — your advisor confirms exact figures at your free consultation.
Who decides if my student is admitted?Decided by: the college
We prepare the application, transcripts, and supporting materials — but the college's admissions office makes the call.
Who decides on the visa?Decided by: the consulate
The U.S. Consulate, at interview. We run mock interviews and prepare documentation, but we don't control the outcome.
Who decides if a transfer credit counts?Decided by: the university
The receiving university's registrar, per its published transfer agreement — which is why we only build 2+2 pathways around named, real transfer systems.
Who decides on transfer/status approval after enrollment?Decided by: USCIS / CBP
U.S. immigration authorities. We monitor status and flag issues early, but the decision is theirs.
What does Eurasia Foundation decide?Decided by: us
Whether a pathway is the right fit for your student. If it isn't, we'll say so — and point you toward one that is, even if that means EducationUSA instead of us.
What happens if we're told "no" somewhere along the way?Decided by: varies
A "no" is never a dead end in our process. We route back to reviewing options and build a new preparation plan with you.

Why a foundation is doing this.

For thirty years, Eurasia Foundation has worked directly in the communities it serves — not as a distant funder, but as a partner on the ground. American Academic Horizons grew out of a simple observation: talented students across Central Asia have real paths to U.S. colleges, and smaller U.S. colleges have real room for them, but almost nobody is connecting the two honestly. So we built the connection ourselves, using the same standards of transparency and accountability that have defined our work since 1994.
